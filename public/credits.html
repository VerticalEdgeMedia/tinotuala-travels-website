Credits

Pictures, sounds, makers and licences.

Every picture on this mock-up comes from Wikimedia Commons under a licence that allows reuse: a public-domain painting or engraving, a CC0 release, or a CC BY / CC BY-SA photograph. Each one is listed below with its maker, its licence and a link to the original file page. The same information is kept as data in img/_credits.json.

The photographs are stand-ins chosen to show the layout. They are not photographs of Tino's trips, and nobody pictured is a client. When Tino supplies his own images, these come out and the list shrinks with them.

The two photographs of Tino in the About section were supplied by Tino Tuala himself. They are listed at the foot of this page for completeness, and they are not part of the freely licensed set.

54 pictures and 4 sound files are published on this site, and 54 pictures and 4 sound files are credited here.

Animal recordings

Four calls, heard roughly every fifteen seconds once you have checked in, and never before. They are trimmed and re-encoded from the Commons originals. The delay, the reverb and the pitch drop are generated live in the browser and are not part of the recordings. There is always a visible sound switch, and muted means silent.

  1. Mantled howler monkey (Alouatta palliata) calls, Santa Rosa National Park, Costa Rica

    Recorded by Richard Ranft (sound recordist); The British Library Board. Licensed CC BY 4.0. File page on Wikimedia Commons.

    animal-monkey.mp3 and animal-monkey.ogg · 3.5 seconds · used in: Ambience, the monkey call

    Trimmed, normalised and converted to mono mp3 and ogg. cut from 00:01.0 to 00:04.5 of the original

  2. Elephant trumpeting

    Recorded by தகவலுழவன (Thagavaluzhavan). Licensed CC0. File page on Wikimedia Commons.

    animal-elephant.mp3 and animal-elephant.ogg · 1.3 seconds · used in: Ambience, the elephant call

    Trimmed, normalised and converted to mono mp3 and ogg. cut from 00:00.08 to 00:01.38 of the original (the whole trumpet; the source is only 1.44 s long)

  3. Tiger growling and snarling (recorded in a cage, natural reverb)

    Recorded by schots. Licensed CC0. File page on Wikimedia Commons.

    animal-bigcat.mp3 and animal-bigcat.ogg · 3.2 seconds · used in: Ambience, the big cat call

    Trimmed, normalised and converted to mono mp3 and ogg. cut from 00:22.0 to 00:25.2 of the original

  4. Laughing kookaburra (Dacelo novaeguineae) call

    Recorded by Powys, V., Australian National Wildlife Collection (ANWC X00520). Licensed CC BY 3.0. File page on Wikimedia Commons.
